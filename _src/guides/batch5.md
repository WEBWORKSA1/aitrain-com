=== ai-for-real-estate-agents | AI for real estate agents: listings, follow-ups and market updates | Role Guides | 7 | How real estate agents can use AI to write listings, nurture leads, prepare market updates and save admin time — while staying accurate and compliant.
Real estate runs on communication: listings, follow-ups, market updates and paperwork. AI can draft most of it in minutes, freeing agents for the work that needs a person — showings, negotiation and relationships.

## Key takeaways

- AI is excellent for first drafts of listings, emails and social posts.
- Every property fact must come from you or the listing data, not the AI.
- Fair-housing and advertising rules apply to AI-written copy just as they do to yours.
- Keep client personal information out of unapproved tools.

## Listing descriptions

```
Write a listing description for the property below. Use only the facts provided; do not add features. 150–200 words, warm but factual, highlight the three strongest features first. Avoid language about the type of people who would suit the home.
Facts: [beds, baths, size, lot, upgrades, neighbourhood amenities, year built]
```

The final instruction matters: describe the property, not the ideal buyer, to avoid language that could raise fair-housing concerns.

## Lead follow-up

- "Write a three-message follow-up sequence for a buyer who attended an open house but hasn't responded. Friendly, no pressure, each under 80 words."
- "Draft a check-in for a past client on their two-year home anniversary."

## Market updates

Paste the actual figures from your board or data source and ask AI to explain them:

> Using only these figures, write a 150-word market update for homeowners in [area]. Explain what changed since last quarter in plain English. Don't predict prices.

## Social and video scripts

- Short scripts for neighbourhood tour videos.
- Caption variations for a new listing across platforms.

## Admin

- Turn showing notes into a client summary.
- Draft checklists for buyers and sellers.
- Summarise long documents you are allowed to share, then verify against the original.

## Compliance reminders

- Check every number and feature against the listing data.
- Follow your licensing body's advertising rules and your brokerage's policy.
- Don't use AI-generated images to misrepresent a property; disclose virtual staging as required.

=== ai-for-marketers | AI for marketers: research, content and campaign workflows | Role Guides | 8 | A practical playbook for marketers using AI for audience research, content production, campaign variants, SEO briefs and reporting — without sounding generic.
Marketing was one of the first professions transformed by generative AI. The teams getting the best results are not the ones producing the most content; they are the ones using AI to think faster and keep quality high.

## Key takeaways

- Use AI for research synthesis, angles and variants — keep strategy and final voice human.
- Feed it real customer language (reviews, interviews, support tickets) for sharper copy.
- Build brand-voice examples into every content prompt.
- Fact-check every claim and statistic.

## Audience research

```
Below are 40 customer reviews (anonymised). Identify the top five jobs customers hire us for, the top five frustrations, and the exact phrases customers use for each. Quote phrases directly from the reviews.
```

Exact customer phrases make better headlines than anything invented.

## Angles and messaging

- "Give me ten different angles for this product aimed at [segment], each with a headline and one-line rationale."
- "Act as a sceptical buyer in [segment]. List the objections you'd have to this landing page."

## Content production

Use [prompt chaining](prompt-chaining-for-long-tasks.html): brief → outline → section drafts → edit. Add two examples of on-brand writing to every drafting prompt.

## Campaign variants

```
Write 5 variants of this ad for A/B testing. Keep the offer identical. Vary only the hook: curiosity, pain point, social proof, specificity, urgency. Max 125 characters each.
```

## SEO briefs

AI can help draft a content brief — search intent, outline, questions to answer, internal links — but check search data in a proper SEO tool. Write for readers first; thin, mass-produced pages tend to perform poorly.

## Reporting

Paste your campaign metrics and ask for a plain-English summary of what changed, what likely caused it, and what to test next. Do calculations in your analytics tool, not in the chat.

## Guardrails

- Disclose AI-generated imagery where your platform or audience expects it.
- Don't publish claims you can't substantiate.
- Check for accidental similarity to competitors' copy.

=== ai-for-hr-teams | AI for HR teams: hiring, onboarding and policy — used responsibly | Role Guides | 8 | How HR professionals can use AI for job descriptions, onboarding, policy writing and employee communications, and why AI must not make decisions about people.
HR teams handle large volumes of writing and repetitive questions, which makes AI attractive. HR also deals with sensitive personal data and consequential decisions, which makes care essential.

## Key takeaways

- Great uses: job descriptions, interview guides, onboarding materials, policy drafts, FAQs.
- Never let AI make or rank decisions about candidates or employees without careful human review and appropriate safeguards.
- Keep personal data out of unapproved tools.
- Check AI-written content for biased or exclusionary language.

## Job descriptions

```
Write a job description for [role] at a [size/type] organisation. Focus on outcomes in the first 6–12 months, then must-have skills (max 6), then nice-to-have skills (max 4). Use inclusive, plain language. Avoid jargon and unnecessary degree requirements.
```

Then ask: "Review this job description for language that may discourage qualified applicants from applying."

## Interview guides

- Structured questions mapped to each must-have skill.
- A scoring guide describing strong, adequate and weak answers.
- Structured interviews are generally fairer and more consistent than unstructured conversations.

## Onboarding

- First-week schedules, checklists and welcome emails.
- Turning long handbooks into role-specific summaries and FAQs (verify accuracy).

## Policies and communications

- Drafting policies from your notes — including an [AI use policy](write-an-ai-use-policy.html).
- Rewriting announcements in clear, empathetic language.
- Translating key communications for multilingual teams (with a native-speaker check where it matters).

## Where to be careful

- **Screening and ranking candidates.** Automated decisions about people can embed bias and may be regulated in some jurisdictions. Treat AI as an assistant to structured human judgement, not a decision-maker.
- **Performance and disciplinary matters.** Keep sensitive details out of general AI tools.
- **Employee data.** Use only approved systems with appropriate agreements.

## HR as the AI-literacy owner

In many organisations, HR leads AI training. A clear programme — foundation for everyone, role-specific workshops, documented completion — supports both productivity and obligations like the [EU AI Act's literacy provision](eu-ai-act-ai-literacy-checklist.html). See [team training](../teams.html).

- Keep client financial data out of unapproved tools.

```
Here is a summary of budget vs actual by department [paste table, no client names]. Write a short commentary for a non-finance manager: the three largest variances, plausible explanations to investigate (labelled as hypotheses), and questions to ask the budget owners. Don't recalculate any figures.
```

=== how-to-choose-an-ai-assistant | How to choose an AI assistant for work: a buyer's checklist | Foundations | 8 | A neutral checklist for comparing AI assistants and AI features for work: capabilities, data terms, admin controls, integrations, cost and fit.
There is no single "best" AI assistant. The right choice depends on the tasks your people do, the software you already use, your data requirements and your budget. This checklist helps you compare options fairly, whatever brands are on your shortlist.

## Key takeaways

- Start from your tasks, then test tools on those tasks.
- Data terms and admin controls matter as much as answer quality for business use.
- AI already built into your existing software may cover many needs.
- Run a short side-by-side trial before committing.

## Step 1: list your top tasks

Write down your ten most important use cases (e.g. drafting client emails, summarising reports, analysing spreadsheets, coding help, research with sources). Test every candidate on the same tasks.

## Step 2: compare capabilities

| Capability | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| Writing quality | Is the output accurate, clear and on-tone for your tasks? |
| Long documents | Can it handle the length of your typical files? |
| Files and data | Can it read spreadsheets, PDFs and images you use? |
| Web search | Can it search and cite current sources when needed? |
| Your knowledge | Can it work with your documents (projects, knowledge bases, RAG)? |
| Integrations | Does it connect with your email, office suite, chat and storage? |
| Coding | If relevant, how well does it handle your languages and tools? |

## Step 3: check data and security

- Is your data used for model training, and can that be disabled?
- Retention and deletion options.
- Data location and agreements available.
- Single sign-on, user management and audit logs.
- Security certifications relevant to your sector.

Always read the current terms; they change.

## Step 4: consider total cost

Licence cost per seat is only part of it. Add training time, administration, and the cost of low adoption. A cheaper tool that nobody uses is the most expensive option.

## Step 5: run a two-week trial

Give a small group two candidates and the same ten tasks. Score each task with a simple rubric (correct, useful, time saved). Combine with a short survey: which would you keep using?

## Step 6: decide and train

Choose, then invest in training — adoption depends far more on people knowing how to use a tool for their own tasks than on small differences between tools. See our [Foundations Line](../paths/foundations.html) and [team training](../teams.html).

=== get-your-employer-to-pay-for-ai-training | How to get your employer to pay for AI training (with email template) | AI for Leaders | 6 | A practical way to ask your manager to fund AI training — the business case, a ready-to-send email template, and how to report results afterwards.
Many organisations have training budgets that go unused, and most managers want their teams to use AI well. The key is framing your request around business results, not personal interest.

## Key takeaways

- Tie the training to specific tasks and measurable time savings.
- Offer to share what you learn with the team.
- Keep the request short and include cost, time and expected benefit.
- Report back afterwards — it makes the next request easy.

## Build the case in three numbers

1. **Hours:** how many hours per week you spend on tasks AI could speed up.
2. **Saving:** a conservative estimate of time saved (start with 20–30%).
3. **Cost:** training fee plus your time.

Example: 6 hours/week of reports and emails × 25% saving = 1.5 hours/week ≈ 70 hours/year. That's usually worth far more than the course cost. Try the [ROI calculator](../roi-calculator.html) for your own figures.

## Email template

> **Subject:** Request: AI skills training to speed up [task]
>
> Hi [manager],
>
> I'd like to complete [training name/format] on using AI for [specific tasks, e.g. reporting and client follow-ups]. It takes [hours] and costs [amount].
>
> I currently spend about [X] hours a week on these tasks. Based on what similar teams report, I expect to save at least [Y] hours a week within a month, while following our data and review rules.
>
> Afterwards I'll share a short summary and the prompts that worked best with the team, so the benefit isn't just mine.
>
> Could we approve this from the [team/professional development] budget? Happy to discuss.
>
> Thanks,
> [Name]

## If the answer is "not now"

- Ask what result would justify it, and try free resources first — our [learning lines](../paths.html) are free.
- Suggest a team session instead of individual training; it often fits a different budget line.

## After the training

Send a three-line update after 30 days: what you changed, hours saved, and one example. If the whole team could benefit, point your manager to our [team training options](../teams.html).

Foundations Line

Plain-language grounding in how modern AI works, what large language models are good at, where they fail, and how to use them safely on day one.

Beginner6–8 hours7 stationsFree

Who it's for: Anyone who uses a computer at work and wants to understand what AI can and cannot do before relying on it.

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain in two sentences how a language model produces an answer
  • Pick the right kind of AI tool for a task
  • Spot the three most common failure modes: invented facts, stale knowledge, and overconfidence
  • Write a first useful prompt and improve it with feedback

The route

  1. Station 1: What AI is (and isn't)

    Separate machine learning, deep learning and generative AI, and see why today's assistants feel conversational without being conscious.

  2. Station 2: How language models predict text

    Tokens, context windows and next-token prediction, explained with a paper-and-pencil example you can repeat for a colleague.

  3. Station 3: Strengths and blind spots

    Where models are reliably strong (drafting, summarising, reformatting) and where they need checking (numbers, citations, recent events).

  4. Station 4: Your first ten prompts

    A guided set of everyday tasks with before-and-after prompts so you feel the difference that context makes.

  5. Station 5: Checking AI output

    A five-question verification habit that catches most errors before they reach a client or manager.

  6. Station 6: Privacy at work

    What not to paste into a public chatbot, how enterprise plans differ, and how to read a data-use policy quickly.

  7. Station 7: Choosing your toolkit

    A simple decision table for assistants, search-style tools, image tools and AI features already inside your software.
